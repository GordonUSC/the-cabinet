01 / Survived

What carries across?

02 / Reinvented

What had to change?

03 / Gone

Is its absence felt?

Adaptation

The crossing of a world from one medium into another. The useful question is never whether it is faithful. It is what survived unchanged, what had to be reinvented because the new medium could not carry the original, and what is simply gone.

Example: A Minecraft Movie's prologue beside specific remembered player choices; The Last of Us cold open beside whatever you hold of the game's opening.

What survived

The part of a world that crosses into the new medium unchanged: a name, a color, a silhouette, a rule, a relationship.

Example: Blocks, creatures and the act of building survive into Steve's prologue; look for what else does.

What was reinvented

The part that had to be rebuilt because the new medium cannot carry the original form.

Example: The order and pace of Steve's montage are chosen for you; in the game they are yours.

What is gone

The part that simply does not come along, and the honest judgment of whether its absence is felt.

Example: In A Minecraft Movie you watch someone else build; decide whether the prologue aches for it, and say why from a frame.

What the audience knows first

A scene that hands the audience a piece of knowledge before the characters have it, so ordinary scenes that follow carry weight the characters cannot feel.

Example: The Last of Us cold open, up to the title card, at 10:45.

Reference, not decoration

An image on a mood board that answers a question the game will actually ask: how it moves, how it lights, how a screen reads, what the player's hands do.

Example: A team adds one frame that shows who builds and how the camera watches it, and replaces one image it could not justify.
