A vocabulary for crossings

Survived, reinvented,
gone.

Six terms, each with an example, a counterexample and a place to look.

1What survived the crossing unchanged?
2What had to be reinvented, because the new medium could not carry the original?
3What is simply gone, and is its absence felt?

Course glossary, Session 12

Adaptation

The crossing of a world from one medium into another. The useful question is never whether it is faithful. It is what survived unchanged, what had to be reinvented because the new medium could not carry the original, and what is simply gone.

Never a question of faithfulness. Three questions instead: what survived, what was reinvented, what is gone.

Example: Riot's Awaken cinematic against the Arcane trailer. Same company, same world, and a visual language you would never guess was related.

Not this: A remake in the same medium is not an adaptation; nothing had to cross.

Where to look: Awaken then Arcane, 10:12 to 10:19, and again with Minecraft at 11:16.

The first question

What survived

The part of a world that crosses into the new medium unchanged: a name, a color, a silhouette, a rule, a relationship.

Usually smaller than fans expect. It is what the audience would riot over losing.

Example: Jinx's silhouette and Piltover's split city survive from League into Arcane; the rendering style does not.

Not this: A reference or a cameo is not survival; it is a wink.

Where to look: Any frame you can name the source of without being told.

The second question

What was reinvented

The part that had to be rebuilt because the new medium cannot carry the original form.

A game's loop becomes a plot. A stage's single wide view becomes a cut. A player's choice becomes a character's decision.

Example: Minecraft has no protagonist; a film needs one, so the movie invents people to watch.

Not this: Changing something the new medium could have carried is a choice, not a reinvention; say which it is.

Where to look: The Minecraft pair, 11:16.

The third question, the hard one

What is gone

The part that simply does not come along, and the honest judgment of whether its absence is felt.

Often the thing that made the original itself: your freedom to look anywhere on a stage, your authorship of every block.

Example: In Hamilton on screen the camera chooses where you look. In A Minecraft Movie you watch someone else build.

Not this: "It is different" is not an answer. Name the missing thing and say whether the work aches without it.

Where to look: Your team's written answer at 10:48, and the board talk at 11:30.

Craft term, Session 12

In-engine cinematography

Cinema authored inside a game engine: lighting, camera and composition built in the same tool that runs the game.

The film language arrives without the film apparatus. No set, no crane, no golden hour to wait for. What stays hard: faces, hands, weight, the accident a real camera would have caught.

Example: The Clair Obscur launch trailer and GTA VI Trailer 1, both at 11:46.

Not this: A pre-rendered cinematic made in an offline renderer is not in-engine; it borrows the engine's world and none of its constraints.

Where to look: Any shot where the camera moves like a film camera through a game space.

Studio term, today at 12:15

Finishing pass

A critique that adds nothing and removes what is weakest: count the images, say the palette, name the weakest image and cut it.

Seven minutes a team, board on the projector, three passes, nothing else.

Example: A board at 14 images finds the problem it can fix tonight. A board at 41 loses one it was too fond of.

Not this: Adding three good images to cover one bad one is not a finishing pass; the bad one is still there.

Where to look: Your own board, 12:15 to 12:45.
