A vocabulary for looking

Evidence, attention,
and control.

Environmental storytelling and agency are working concepts for today. Visual Hierarchy is a review from the course glossary. Palette, composition, lighting, framing and pacing remain useful tools.

Try the demonstrations

Working concept for today

Environmental storytelling

A space and its objects give us evidence from which to infer events, habits or relationships.

Placement, wear, absence and access can all matter. A plausible interpretation is not a confirmed fact.

Example: In the workshop lab, two workstations, paired controls and one empty seat suggest work arranged around more than one person.

Not an example: Calling a room lonely without pointing to anything that creates that reading.

Demonstrate: Observe, then Reveal evidence

Look again: Edith Finch 0:50-5:00: separate visible objects and actions from what the voice supplies.

Review from Session 6

Visual Hierarchy

The order in which a composition makes elements noticeable through contrast, scale, placement and other visual choices.

Attention can be guided without locking the camera. What is most visible is not automatically the most meaningful.

Example: In the lab, raise contrast around the paired controls while keeping every object in the same place.

Not an example: Adding labels that tell viewers the answer while leaving the visual arrangement unchanged.

Demonstrate: Attention: neutral versus controls

Look again: Hades II trailer: nominate a specific frame and explain what attracts your eye first.

Working concept for today

Agency

The ability to make meaningful choices in an interactive experience, within the options the work gives you.

Choosing where to look differs from changing an outcome. Games can also use fixed cameras and scripted events.

Example: In Explore mode you choose which clue to inspect next. You do not change the workshop's underlying story.

Not an example: Saying any video of gameplay gives its viewer control over the recorded player.

Demonstrate: Explore versus Directed sequence

Look again: Compare a recorded traversal in Edith Finch with a fixed edit; name whose choices you are actually watching.
