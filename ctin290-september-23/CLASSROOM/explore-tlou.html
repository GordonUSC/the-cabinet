Game and screen comparison · An exploration

The Last of Us:
explore, or be shown.

Six stations. Each pairs a Naughty Dog image of the game world with a frame from the HBO series trailer. They share a world; they are not the same scene. For each pair ask: what survived, what was reinvented, what is gone, and say what you checked.

Explore · Choose a station

Your inspection order: none yet.

The first street Boston QZ

Look for: Fences, guards, angular materials, no plants. In the series frame, the same city with nature back in it.

Reveal the creators’ note

“As soon as you walk out onto the street, you see men with guns, fences up everywhere keeping you from walking anywhere other than where the government wants you to be.” Bryant Wilson, Lead Cinematic Animator. “When you play through that, you’re given the option to sit and watch it or just walk on by; it’s not something that we force you to look at.”

Source: Naughty Dog, Building The Last of Us, episode 3 (naughtydog.com, archived copy).

Control and materials Boston QZ

Look for: What is hard and angular. What is soft and growing. Which version makes you feel watched?

Reveal the creators’ note

“The QZ is man trying to control other people, and how to maintain control over the world and over nature. A lot of the materials in the QZ are very angular and hard.” Erick Pangilinan, Art Director. “When we show the QZ, we take out all the plants.”

Source: Naughty Dog, Building The Last of Us, episode 3 (naughtydog.com, archived copy).

Scarcity in the marketplace Boston QZ

Look for: Count what is for sale. Count what is missing. Where does the light come from?

Reveal the creators’ note

“It’s so easy to just clutter that with goods. But, in fact, the goal of that scene was to show the scarcity of living in the QZ and how desperate people were.” Erick Pangilinan. “The lighting in that area is very bluish and very overcast, like you’re in a prison.”

Source: Naughty Dog, Building The Last of Us, episode 3 (naughtydog.com, archived copy).

Warm light through nature Bill's Town

Look for: Where the sun is. What it touches first. Whether danger is visible or only implied.

Reveal the creators’ note

“We wanted to create Bill’s Town in a warm setting, with the sunset shining through nature and the buildings.” Erick Pangilinan. “Bill’s Town is a breath of fresh air, albeit one with danger still lurking in the brush.”

Source: Naughty Dog, Building The Last of Us, episode 3 (naughtydog.com, archived copy).

A person's castle Bill's Town

Look for: Traps, walls, routes. Who controls what comes in and out? Compare a place built by one person with a place seen from the sky.

Reveal the creators’ note

“Bill’s Town has its own isolationist feeling, it’s a person’s castle and it’s his domain. He controls what he lets in and out. And nature is one of those things that can go in and out of it.” Erick Pangilinan.

Source: Naughty Dog, Building The Last of Us, episode 3 (naughtydog.com, archived copy).

Nature takes its course Bill's Town

Look for: In the game you choose the route and the pace. In the trailer the camera chooses for you. What does each choice make you notice?

Reveal the creators’ note

“Everything is either seen through Joel’s perspective or Ellie’s perspective, and that’s it.” Neil Druckmann on the game. “With the show we had this opportunity to unplug from our main characters and plug into these other characters.” Druckmann on the series.

Source: Naughty Dog, Building The Last of Us, episode 3 (naughtydog.com, archived copy).
