Visual Analysis Video Rubric

10% of final grade

One World, Two Media. A 3 to 5 minute video essay in which you take ONE world that exists in two forms and compare how each version does its work. Show both, talk over both, and answer three questions: what survived the crossing unchanged, what had to be reinvented because the new medium could not carry the original, and what is simply gone. That third question is the hard one and it is the one that earns the top band. You may use a pair from the course (The Last of Us game and series, League of Legends and Arcane, Minecraft and the film, Hamilton on stage and on screen) or bring your own, subject to approval in Discord. Screen recording with your own voice over it. The production value is not assessed; the seeing is. Due Thu 24 Sep, 5:00 PM PT.

Visual Observation

5 points · Excellent
Identifies specific camera work, composition, color, lighting, and movement; observations are precise and on-screen annotated
4 points · Good
Names most visual elements; annotation is present but sometimes generic
3 points · Fair
Identifies some visual elements; annotation is minimal
2 points · Below expectation
Vague or missing visual analysis; little or no annotation

Comparative Argument

5 points · Excellent
Names what survived, what was reinvented, and what is gone, with specific on-screen evidence for each. Course terminology is used accurately and naturally throughout. The claim about what is missing is argued rather than asserted
4 points · Good
All three questions answered with evidence; terminology accurate; the argument about absence is present but thinner
3 points · Fair
Two of the three questions answered; comparison is mostly description rather than argument; some terminology misused
2 points · Below expectation
Describes two works without comparing them, or compares without evidence; little or no terminology

Scoring: Total possible = 10 points. Two criteria × 5 points each. Averaged if multiple videos are submitted.

Mood Board Rubric

20% of final grade (Group Project 1)

The prompt: a game involving collaboration. Not co-op bolted onto a shooter, a game whose subject is people needing each other. Every team gets the same six words. Your team curates a mood board of 20 to 40 images establishing the visual language, tone, color palette, and world of that game, then presents it to the class for five minutes on Mon 5 Oct. Everyone on the team speaks. This is Group Project 1 and it belongs to your FIRST team, which dissolves once this is delivered; you are reassigned to your final-project team on 5 Oct. File due Thu 1 Oct, 5:00 PM PT, which is the Thursday before presentations because the Thursday of presentation week is fall recess.

Visual Cohesion

5 points · Excellent
Images communicate a clear, unified aesthetic direction; color, tone, and composition support a compelling vision
4 points · Good
Mostly cohesive; some variation but generally aligned vision
3 points · Fair
Some coherence; vision is present but loose or unclear
2 points · Below expectation
Disjointed; lacks clear visual direction or unity

Curation & Justification

5 points · Excellent
Each image is thoughtfully chosen and relevant to project vision; team can articulate why each image belongs
4 points · Good
Images are mostly well-chosen; justification present but sometimes brief
3 points · Fair
Some images feel tangential; limited justification provided
2 points · Below expectation
Images seem arbitrary; no clear justification for selections

Color & Palette Development

5 points · Excellent
Establishes a sophisticated color language; palette is distinctive and supports project concept
4 points · Good
Color palette is clear and functional; mostly supports concept
3 points · Fair
Color is considered but somewhat generic or safe
2 points · Below expectation
Color palette is unclear or underdeveloped

Presentation & Accessibility

5 points · Excellent
Well-organized, labeled, and presented for easy navigation and understanding; high visual presentation quality
4 points · Good
Clear organization; mostly accessible; good presentation
3 points · Fair
Readable but somewhat disorganized; basic presentation
2 points · Below expectation
Difficult to navigate; poor presentation quality

Scoring: Total possible = 20 points. Four criteria × 5 points each. All team members receive the same grade.
