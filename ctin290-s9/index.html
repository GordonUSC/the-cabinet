Second Text, Hamilton, and the Camera as the Thing You Lost

Critical Role gives you performance with an unmoving camera and four hours to breathe. Hamilton is a stage show crossed onto a screen, and the trailer is where the cost of that crossing becomes visible. In a theater your eye chooses, and you can watch the person who is not speaking. On a screen somebody chose for you. Ask what that gains, because it does gain something real: close-ups no seat in the house could ever buy. Then ask what it costs. This is the first appearance of the Adaptation through-line you name properly in Session 12, and it lands harder here, straight after an hour of watching performance with no director's eye in the way.

Exit Ticket, Two Lines Before Anyone Leaves

One term you learned today and where you saw it. One thing you are still unsure of. Collect them at the door. This costs five minutes and it is the cheapest instrument you have for finding out what actually landed. The two things students are least likely to volunteer are which concept went past them and what they believe is expected of them, and this asks for both directly. Read them before the next session and open with the most common confusion, by name, so the room learns that saying you are lost actually changes what happens.

Say these four things out loud

  • At 10:00. "Four hundred and fifty hours of performance, one camera that barely moves. By noon you will be able to tell these people apart with the sound off."
  • At 11:16, opening Hamilton. "In a theater your eye chooses. On a screen somebody chose for you. What did that buy, and what did it cost?"
  • Whenever you name a term. Show it on screen first, define it second. All three of today's terms are visible before they are words.
  • At 12:45, collecting the exit tickets. "One term and where you saw it. One thing you are still unsure of. The second line is the one I actually need."

Today's three termsseen before defined, one question each

Blocking

Definition

The choreography of character and camera movement within a scene; physical positioning and trajectory.

What to look at

Bodies at a table. Who leans in, who pulls back, and when.

Where you see it

Sample any 10 min
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Mbynm0pGX0

Ask the room

Who moved last, and what did that movement tell you before anybody spoke?

Gesturality

Definition

The expressive quality of movement; how characters move and what that movement communicates about state, intent, or personality.

What to look at

One performer, many selves, distinguished almost entirely by how they move.

Where you see it

Throughout, 2:26
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wxN1T1uxQ2g

Ask the room

If you muted this, how many of these people could you still tell apart?

Reaction Shot

Definition

A shot of a character responding to action; often cut in for emotional emphasis or continuity.

What to look at

The table's faces are the story as often as the speaker is.

Where you see it

Sample any 10 min
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Mbynm0pGX0

Ask the room

Whose face is the story right now, and is it the person talking?

The 10:12 block cannot run as written, and this is the one thing to fix before you walk in

"Watch CR opening 30 min" points at a video that is 1 minute 26 seconds long. Uo9z2gIUmUo is a clip called The Tables Converge. Open it at 10:12 and you have eighty-six seconds and then thirty-four minutes of nothing in front of twelve people.

Play 3Mbynm0pGX0 instead. That is The Fall of Thjazi Fang, Campaign 4, Episode 1, four hours and twenty-seven minutes, the actual episode with the actual character introductions in it. Same official channel. Durations checked twice tonight, two different methods, same answers.

Scrub it before 10:00 and write the number here, because a cold scrub in front of the room is four minutes you do not have: the episode proper starts at _______ and you stop at _______.

Hamilton is sixty seconds and the block is thirty minutes. That is not a problem, it is the shape of the block: play it three times. Cold. Again muted, so they watch only where the cuts land. Then a third time with the room calling each cut out loud. Ninety seconds of tape, twenty-eight minutes of argument.

Two smaller ones. "Action Sequences" is the same 1:26 clip under a different name, so there is no third Critical Role video. And the glossary calls 3Mbynm0pGX0 "Key Character Moments" and says "sample any 10 min" with no timestamp, which is a four and a half hour video; pick your ten minutes tonight, not at the podium.
