NOW WATCH THE COURSE MATERIAL

Who gets to move?
Who gets the frame?

Hamilton makes blocking easier to see. Track where performers stand, which paths they take, who faces whom, and how distance changes. Then ask how the filmed view directs your attention within that arrangement.

Critical Role makes small performance choices easier to inspect. At the table, notice hands, posture, gaze and listening while someone else speaks. A visible reaction is not automatically a reaction shot: look for framing or a cut that emphasizes the person responding.

Why this lab comes first: it isolates one choice at a time. In the course footage, staging, gesture, sound and editing work together. Both works can contain all three techniques; neither belongs to just one term.

Terms, teaching notes and sources

Blocking: the planned arrangement and movement of performers in space, including their relationship to the camera. This controlled example locks the camera to make the performers’ choices easier to compare.

Gesturality: how bodily gestures contribute to expression. This exercise does not supply an emotion for each movement.

Reaction shot: a view that directs attention to someone’s response. This simplified illustration shows what becomes visible, not the full subtlety of human acting.

Related professional instruction: FilmSkills · Blocking Actors and Filmmakers Academy · Staging Positions.
