Show all questions and answers as a list
  1. Vonnie: Gordon, welcome. GTA VI has been out a week now. What's your verdict?Biggest entertainment launch ever reported: $3.79 billion on day one, by Take-Two's own estimate. But the bigger story is cultural. One game got the whole world to show up on the same day. That almost never happens anymore, and Rockstar did it with patience: thirteen years and one world built with intent.
  2. Paul: Help investors understand that $3.79 billion. How much of it is real money for Take-Two?Good question, because it's consumer spend, not Take-Two revenue. Stores and platforms take a cut, and it likely includes pre-orders. The real tell is the next three years. GTA V kept selling for over a decade because people stayed in the world. For VI, watch month six, not day one, and watch when the online world arrives.
  3. TRAP · Paul: Record launch, but the stock sold off. Why? Is Take-Two a buy here?The stock is the analysts' call, and full disclosure, Take-Two supports scholarships at my nonprofit. What I see is that most of this business is what players spend after they buy the game, and GTA VI's online world hasn't launched yet. The launch proved the audience is there. The next chapter is whether they stay.
  4. Vonnie: Thirteen years in development. Is that a model or a warning for the industry?Both. It's a model for Rockstar and a warning for everyone else. Most studios can't wait thirteen years and shouldn't try. What they can copy is the discipline: make one place feel real before you make ten places look pretty.
  5. Vonnie: What can smaller studios learn from Rockstar without matching its scale?Three things travel at any budget. Make one place feel real before you make ten feel pretty. Write for what players do, not for the trailer. And trust that audiences can tell when a person made something. My students at USC ship games in weeks with four people. Same rules.
  6. Paul: Rockstar built this without generative AI. Is that sustainable as AI gets better?I'm Director of Ethics at USC's School of Cinematic Arts, so I live this question. Take-Two's CEO said gen AI had zero part in this game. The line isn't AI or no AI. It's whose judgment is on screen. AI can help people build faster. It can't decide what's worth building. Rockstar put a person behind every corner, and players feel it.
  7. Vonnie: It's Black Friday. Does GTA VI suck the oxygen out of holiday spending?It concentrates it. Families have a games budget, and this year a big piece of it has one name on it. It's selling consoles too: US PS5 sales ran more than double a year ago in the week before launch. So it lifts hardware and squeezes the mid-size games launching around it.
  8. Paul: Eighty dollars. Is that the new industry standard?For a world people will live in for years, players have decided it's worth it. Per hour, it's still the cheapest entertainment in the house. The risk is for games that charge eighty and deliver a weekend.
  9. TRAP · Vonnie: There's a union vote at Rockstar this week. How do you see the labor side?Thirteen years is a long time to ask of people. The best games come from teams that can sustain the work, so I'd want every studio, including this one, listening to its people. That's how you get the next masterpiece, not just this one.
  10. Paul: You helped make Madden. How has the business changed since then?When I worked on Madden '95, games were a teenage hobby. Now one game outsells Hollywood's biggest weekend in a single day. What hasn't changed is that people want to see themselves in the world. That's why I run Gay Gaming Professionals: our excellence is universal, and our lens helps the industry see who it still underestimates.
  11. Vonnie: We're almost out of time. Last word, Gordon: what are you watching next?Three things. Whether players stay when the online world arrives. Whether thirteen years of patience changes how other studios plan. And the human-made bar Rockstar just set. Every game that ships with AI inside it will be measured against it.
