Know Your Team, Then Make

Monday, October 12, 10:00 AM to 12:50 PM. Today your team agrees how it works together, then starts the 30-second piece you present on October 21.

The one question: how does time pass in your world, and can you make an audience feel it without saying it? A prototype proves one idea, not the whole piece. Throwaway is fine. A prototype that fails has done its job: better to fail in week eight than in week fourteen.

Running order

10:00Standup, standing up. Two minutes a team, three things only: what moved since last session, what is blocked, what you will have done by next time. Blockers go on the board.
10:15Team culture, 15 minutes. Choose privately: quiet thinking or talking it through, written or spoken feedback, exploring or planning. Share what helps you contribute, in whatever mode you like. Passing is fine. Then write one agreement: "We value ___; when ___, we will ___; evidence will be ___." Apply it to one real frame, sketch or timeline choice.
10:3045-second share-out, every team. "We want our team to feel ___. Our agreement is ___. Here is the creative choice it helped us make. Our next artifact is ___, owned by ___. We need help with ___." Show the artifact.
10:36The first milestone. What you present October 21 and submit October 22. Pick your track and technique.
10:42Track production. Mentors rotate with real-time feedback.
10:52Ten-minute break. Take all ten and leave the room.
11:02Team project time. Fifteen minutes with Gordon per team, in a fixed order: show the build running, name the biggest risk to the deadline, agree one thing that will be true by next session.
12:02Screening and feedback. Show where your 30 seconds stands, even rough. What worked, and what is the next step?
12:35Blockers out loud. Which moved, which did not. One sentence per team on what will be true by next session.
