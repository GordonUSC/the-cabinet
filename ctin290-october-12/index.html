Team culture exercise

What you are trying to do: learn how each teammate works best, turn that into one simple team rule, and use the rule on a real decision for your 30-second piece. Teams that talk about this early argue less later and make better work.

Step 1
3 min		Think on your own. Write down how you like to work. Pick one from each pair, or write your own: think quietly first or talk it out; get feedback in writing or out loud; try lots of ideas or plan first.
Why: you get a minute to decide before anyone else's opinion pulls you along.
Step 2
3 min		Tell your team. Each person finishes this sentence: "I do my best work when..." Say it, write it, or show it. You can skip anything personal.
Why: you can't plan around a teammate's needs until you know them.
Step 3
5 min		Make one team rule. Fill in: "We care about ___. So when ___ happens, we will ___. We'll know it's working when ___." Then use the rule on one real choice for your 30 seconds: a shot, a sketch, or a moment on the timeline. Write down what you chose and what you said no to.
Why: a rule only counts if it changes how you make something.
Step 4
3 min		Check everyone agrees. Each person says "works for me," "needs a change," or "let's talk after." Staying quiet does not count as yes. Fix the rule if anyone needs a change.
Why: a rule only one person agreed to will fall apart in week ten.
Step 5
1 min		Name the next step. What you will make next, who owns it, who helps, and what might get in the way.
Why: you leave with a real job, not just a good talk.

Share-out, 45 seconds: "We want our team to feel ___. Our rule is ___. It helped us choose ___. Next, ___ is making ___, with help from ___. We could use help with ___." Show what you made.

Two example answers

Made-up teams, to show the shape

Example 1: Team Tide (Animation) How they work: two like to think quietly first, two like to talk it out. Three want written feedback.
Rule: "We care about everyone's ideas getting heard. So when we pick a shot, everyone sketches one first, alone, for five minutes. We'll know it's working when every sketch is on the wall before anyone votes."
Real choice: for a tide going out over one day, they chose one fixed camera with the water dropping, over a fast montage, because the slow change makes you feel the hours.
Next step: Maya animates the first 5 seconds of the water by Wednesday; Jon helps with timing. Risk: render time.
Example 2: Team Afterglow (Game/Interactive) How they work: three like trying lots of ideas, one likes a plan. Everyone prefers feedback out loud.
Rule: "We care about trying things fast. So when we disagree, we build both versions for 20 minutes and play them. We'll know it's working when we pick from what we played, not from who argued best."
Real choice: to show a day passing in a room, they tested the light moving across the floor against a clock on the wall. They kept the light, because players noticed time without being told.
Next step: Sam builds the light cycle in engine by Wednesday; Priya records it. Risk: the light looks flat.
