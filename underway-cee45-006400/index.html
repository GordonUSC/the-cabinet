A game for the 45th anniversary of the Center for Excellence in Education · 1983 to 2028

Underway

Every Rickoid's summer, played together. A thank-you to Ms. D.
A concept by Gordon Bellamy, RSI '87, with Theresa Loong's film · Draft for Ms. D and the CEE team · November 2026
In one minute

Underway is a warm, easy game that lets anyone at the 45th Reunion live an RSI summer: a week of classes, five weeks in the lab with a mentor, and Presentation Day. It plays on the big screen at the reunion and on any phone, with one button and no gaming experience needed.

Every round adds a light to a map of 63 nations and all 50 states, one for every Rickoid. It ends at Ms. D's holiday party, where alumni from every decade say thank you in their own voices.

How it plays

The game follows the shape of an RSI summer, so every alum knows where they are, and every guest learns what that summer means.

The $250 Seed

Prologue: The $250 Seed

2 minutes · anyone · one tap

A single coin. One tap plants it, and time-lapse carries it from 1983 to today: the first RSI class in 1984, the first international students, Bulgaria, Shanghai, Stanford. The tree grows a branch for every class year.

What it should feel like: "Look how far one good idea traveled."

Week One

Act I: Week One, Classes

3 minutes · learn the one move

Quick, playful lessons with professors teach the game's one move: connect an idea to evidence. Every answer earns a notebook page, and nothing is ever failed.

"I can do this."

The Lab

Act II: Five Weeks in the Lab

10 minutes · the heart of the game

Each week is a round. You pick a mentor card, run experiments, and grow two numbers: Insight and Rigor. They multiply only at the end of the week, so careful choices pay off big. Week four is the week the experiment fails, as it always does. You choose: start over, or find what the failure is telling you. Both paths can win.

"That surprise taught me something."

Presentation Day

Act III: Presentation Day

3 minutes · the standing ovation

Present to a panel. Your notebook becomes a poster, the panel asks one good question, and the room applauds. Your poster joins a wall with every player's, and your light joins the map.

"I made something real, and it counts."

The Party

The hub: Ms. D's Holiday Party

Always open · stay as long as you like

The game's home is a warm living room at the holiday party Rickoids know. Guests from every decade share stories, and each story is an ornament on the tree. The finale is a wall of thank-you messages, recorded by alumni in their own voices, for Ms. D and the CEE team.

"We are one family across 45 years."

Made so Ms. D enjoys it

One button, big type, no rush. Every screen works with a single tap, and text is large and high contrast.

A "Just watch" mode. The whole story can be enjoyed like a film, with a guest playing for her or with her.

Nothing to lose. No game over. Every path reaches Presentation Day and the party.

It is about her people. The finale is the community saying thank you to her and her team.

The design, for game makers

Built with MDA (mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics) and Bartle's four player types, so experts, newcomers, talkers and competitors all find their place.

Aesthetic (the feeling)Dynamic (what happens)Mechanic (the rule)
FellowshipStrangers compare their summers and find each otherPass-the-device play, class-year tags, story ornaments
DiscoveryEvery run uncovers another decade of CEE historyMap lights, unlockable class-year rooms, archive cards
ChallengeCareful choices beat lucky ones in the labInsight x Rigor, multiplied at week's end; the week-four failure
ExpressionEvery poster is personalChoose a field, mentor and title; posters saved to the wall

Achievers

An Excellence score, a "Rickoid of the Year" badge for the best run of each night, and the Double Rickoid challenge (lab plus a biology bonus round).

Explorers

45 years to uncover: class-year rooms, the map of 63 nations, archive cards from CEE's history.

Socializers

The holiday party hub, story ornaments, pass the phone to a friend, and the thank-you wall.

Competitors

Friendly class-versus-class rallies on the big screen ("'87 vs '99"), with every point lighting the shared map. Rivalry that only adds light.

Two looks to choose from

A. The Lab Notebook (recommended)

Everything drawn on graph paper, in pencil and colored ink, like the notebook every Rickoid kept that summer. Warm, readable, and personal.

B. The Constellation

A night sky over the campuses, every Rickoid a star. Majestic, and the natural finale for the map, even if A is the main look.

Design review, honestly

Status: a first playable exists as of November 2026. It has been tested by script on a phone and a desktop in two browsers, and not yet with people, so how it feels is still unknown.

Biggest risk: stories and consent. The game is only as warm as what alumni share, and every real name needs a yes.

First experiment: five alumni from different decades play the first playable while we watch, to see whether the Lab is fun and whether the summer feels true.

Still to gather: lines from Ms. D's book Rickover: Underway, My Way, and RSI traditions only alumni know. Gordon's own RSI '87 memories come first.
