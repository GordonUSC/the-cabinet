Baku, explained by three circles and two bands.

Trent, this is your edition: the same day, drawn as geometry. A circuit is three shapes and a legend. Gordon is on your couch. Nothing below knows how it went.

David's editionShared programme

The black circle is the Old City. The castle section, Turns 8 to 10, runs along its wall. Nobody passes there. Everybody can crash there.
The vermilion circle is the safety car probability, 57 percent. It sits inside the city because that is where it is born.
The ochre arch is the Flame Towers, and the ochre in the legend below is McLaren.
The long black band is the shoreline straight, 2.2 kilometers, 220 miles an hour, the one real passing place.
The short red band is Russell's pole margin, 0.837 seconds, the largest of his career.

Five facts, as shapes.

P16

Where the championship leader starts. Kimi Antonelli, 81 points clear with eight wins, hit the Turn 1 wall in Q1. A safety car hands him the race back for free; a green race is a 51 lap climb.

0.837

Seconds between Russell on pole and Leclerc in second. The Race called it Mercedes' most dominant performance of 2026. Mercedes owns the straight; Ferrari and McLaren own the castle.

0.001

Seconds between Leclerc and Piastri, two philosophies of car separated by a millisecond.

P8

Verstappen, fresh engine after an ICE failure, a yellow flag on his last lap, and a verdict on his own team: "not good enough." One of two drivers who has won here more than once.

And one more without a number: the first Saturday race in Baku's history, moved for the Day of Remembrance, with 2026 cars carrying active aerodynamics, so the old DRS train down the straight is gone. One stop, all three compounds in play.
