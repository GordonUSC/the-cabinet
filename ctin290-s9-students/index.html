The three terms

Each one gets seen on screen before it gets defined. Here they are anyway, so you can arrive already looking for them.

01

Blocking

Where people are standing, and where they move. Position plus trajectory, doing work before anybody speaks.

Look for: who leans in, who pulls back, and who chose their spot before the scene started. The person who does not move is often the one who decided.

0:37:43 Critical Role, two characters walk uphill to a door

02

Gesturality

How a body moves, and what the movement tells you about who this is, before the dialogue confirms it.

Look for: one performer playing several people. If you muted it, how many of them could you still tell apart?

0:37 to 0:52 Everything Everywhere All At Once, the same man twice

03

Reaction shot

A cut to somebody who is not talking. It is how an edit tells you what a line meant.

Look for: whose face is the story right now, and whether it is the person speaking. Usually it is not.

0:27 to 0:41 Hamilton, two things happening at once

The question the whole day is built on

In a theater, your eye chooses. You can watch the person who is not speaking. On a screen, somebody already chose for you.

So: what did that choice buy, and what did it cost? It buys you a close-up no seat in the house could ever get. Name the gain first. Then name what you lost, because that is the harder half and it is the half that earns the top band on Thursday.

  2. Your team mood board FILE DUE THU 1 OCT, 5:00 PM PT · 20% · presentations MON 5 OCT

    Bring it today. The last forty minutes of class are yours to work on it with the room, and there is no other team time on the calendar before it is due.

    Your board is where the terms stop being a list and start being something you used. Every image on it should be there for a reason you can say in the vocabulary: this one is here for its negative space, this one for the warm/cool split, this one because the blocking tells you who has the power. "It feels right" is worth nothing on 5 October. The same observation in the vocabulary is worth the grade, and you will each be speaking for about a hundred seconds, so the words have to be ready.

    Three worked examples are at the mood board page. Every plate there names the terms it demonstrates, and each board shows how many of the terms you own it actually puts to work. Check yours against that row before you submit. There are also two model presentations, five to six minutes, three speakers, timed to the second.

  3. The terms NOT GRADED ON ITS OWN · UNDERNEATH EVERYTHING ELSE

    Fifty-five of them by December. Thirty-two by the time the board is due, three of them today. They are the reason the video and the board get marks, because a critique that says "the lighting is really good" has not said anything, and one that says "the key light drops two stops when he lies" has. Learning them is not a separate task you do after the work. It is the work.
