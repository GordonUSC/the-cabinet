For Camilla Benbow

Dear Camilla and David,

Thank you for a lifetime of research that takes lives like mine seriously, and for making time for me this summer. You asked me to send media that represents my past, present and future. 02 · WHAT THIS IS NOT

And what it is not

  • Not SMPY data. It holds no scores and makes no claim about the cohort, about talent in general, or about what one life proves. One life is one life.
  • Not a request. There is no reply expected, no funding, no meeting, and no tracking. This page makes no network requests except to links you choose to open.
  • Yours to cite. Quote it or cite any part of it however serves your work, and please ask me anything about any entry. I welcome every question.
  • Yours to decline. Delete it at any time, for any reason, without a word.

Note two02

For Camilla

You have been Dean of Peabody College since 1998, and, alongside David, a steward of a study that has followed children for more than five decades. In your 2024 interview on your reappointment you spoke of a talent development lens, and of AI, social entrepreneurship and taking Peabody to the world (The Vanderbilt Hustler).

I work in that neighborhood. I teach, I lead ethics for a film and games school, and I help run a nonprofit scholars program that looks for ability where it is easy to overlook and tries to attach opportunity to it. I offer that as context, not as a pitch.

Note three03

For David

Three ideas from your work sit closest to what this record shows: that ability is a pattern and not a single number, that interests and preferences shape where ability goes, and that how people allocate their time is part of how they compose a meaningful life. I have not tried to apply them to myself. The record supplies dated outcomes and leaves the interpretation, and the scores, to you.

Games are a spatial, technical and social craft, and I have not found SMPY writing about them. If one life in that field is ever useful to you, this is one, and you are welcome to use it or ignore it.

One small coincidence. Nature notes that you wrestled. In February 1992 I covered Harvard's wrestling team for The Crimson ("Grapplers Drop Two More Contests"). The team lost. I hope the prose held up better.

Note four04

In my own words

I have carried your study with me for most of my life. I still point people to the Nature piece and the documentary you sent, and when I read your paper on age 50 I recognized my own cohort in it. It is a rare thing to be taken seriously by a body of science.

What I did with that: I built a career where games and education meet. I led the IGDA, I teach at USC, I lead ethics for a film and games school, and I co-founded a nonprofit that pays its scholars directly and looks for ability where it is easy to overlook.

If a participant's view is ever useful, here is mine. Your brief says that exceptional talent exists in unexpected places, and I have seen it. David has said these children often need earlier access to what is already available. I would only add that talent is easier to see when the people looking have been taught to see it, so I try to teach the people who do the looking.

Family, for me, is chosen and durable: Joe, my son Brennan, and friends I have known for forty years. They taught me that a trajectory is a shared thing, and that no one gets there alone.

Now I am trying to make AI useful for the same reason, and to keep people in the picture. Thank you for taking these lives seriously. I am glad to be one of them.

Gordon Bellamy

Method

How this was made, and what I left out

I prepared this with an AI research assistant that gathered and cross-checked sources, and I reviewed every entry. Where sources disagreed, I printed the most conservative wording or left the detail out. A person cannot independently verify their own life, so the entries marked My own account should be read as exactly that. Corrections are welcome and I will thank you for them.

On purpose, this leaves out my test scores, my health, my finances, and details about other people. Those are mine to share if I ever choose to, and only with the consent of the people involved. The one exception is the optional panel about identity, closed by default.
