A DESIGN CRITIQUE · ASTRA + DARBY · OCTOBER 2026

Make the welcome
part of the play.

The worlds already had heart. Their entrances asked a new friend to do too much work before that heart became visible.

Our interpretation of Gordon’s through-line is making room for people: a dance floor, a classroom, a game, a shared story. The redesign gives each visitor a small way to participate before asking them to explore the full history.

Two people at the door

An EDM-loving friend

“Give me the feeling, then give me a night we could actually share.”

Shows with Friends had wonderful real photography and playful instruments, but a large roster and a stale “next up” date made the first useful action hard to find. Now the invitation leads to current possibilities, listening links, a personal shortlist, and a plan to copy.

Gordon

“I know my life. Help me see a pattern and make the next connection.”

A biography alone repeats what Gordon already knows. The new entrance offers an interpretation: shows look ahead, the media log opens the public conversations, Island Hop makes life playable, and the Vanderbilt record takes the long view.

These are design perspectives, not interviews, quotations from actual visitors, or a diagnosis of Gordon’s preferences.

The rubric, set before the changes

PriorityWeightWhat success looks like
First minute25%Understand the invitation, choose a clear action, receive feedback, and accomplish something small.
Usefulness20%Find a plausible next night or a meaningful personal connection; distinguish plans from attendance.
Access and comfort20%Readable phone layout, visible keyboard focus, large primary controls, optional sound and motion, a reading route.
Audiovisual craft15%A clear visual hierarchy; music and feedback reward an action without competing with the text.
Belonging and sharing10%A newcomer understands the context, can share a direct link, and can find the way back.
Agency and replay10%Discovery, accomplishment, social connection, and optional challenge have different paths.

Weights express design priorities. They are not measured visitor outcomes or a claim of accessibility certification.

Mechanics → dynamics → feeling

Shows with Friends

Mechanics: search, filter, listen, save, mix, copy.

Dynamics: move from a mood to a feasible shared plan; return to a saved shortlist.

Aesthetics: anticipation, expression, and fellowship.

The synth is original browser audio. Artist recordings remain clearly separate in their own players.

Island Hop

Mechanics: manual flight or autopilot, island puzzles, stamps, points, a shared home, and an optional circuit.

Dynamics: short successes open a connected personal journey; mastery remains available after the first success.

Aesthetics: discovery, delight, challenge, and belonging.

The live game offers free flight, relaxed flight, a competitive circuit, and a reading route. A simpler guided welcome has been tested in a separate preview.

This uses the MDA framework to connect controls to the experience they create.

Four motivations, four ways to enjoy it

  • Explorers: follow islands, photographs, stories, and source trails. Curiosity should reveal context.
  • Achievers: collect stamps, improve a route, and finish a personal shortlist. Progress is visible.
  • Socialisers: find a possible night with Gordon, copy a plan, or play the two-person circuit together.
  • Competitors: choose the optional Joy Circuit and pursue a better performance. Friendship and gratitude remain outside the score.

These adapt Bartle’s multiplayer-world categories as motivation lenses; visitors can enjoy more than one.

What changed in the first minute

  • Good company: four clear doors, a short optional introduction, a new Higgsfield illustration, optional ElevenLabs music, and the Canva island postcard.
  • Shows with Friends: a clear invitation, the next dated possibilities, search and friend filters, a saved shortlist, a copyable plan, and a 30-second original mix.
  • Island Hop: a return link connects the live game to the four doors. A guided opening with simpler choices, sound off for new visitors, and calm motion has been tested in preview; it is not part of this shared release.
  • Vanderbilt: the complete 97-entry reading edition opens in a browser, with an eight-moment starting path and its sources preserved.
  • Media Log: the shared entrance lands on the watch section and provides a visible return to the four doors.

What we checked

In a local preview in the in-app browser at 390×844, the guided Island Hop puzzle was visible by the 19-second observation. A route was completed at 44 seconds; the passport stamp, story, and 135-point result appeared, and “Fly on” continued to Blizzard. This was an agent walkthrough, not a human usability study.

Shows search, friend filtering, empty-state recovery, saving across reload, copying a plan, and the 30-second mix stopping were exercised. The hub, Shows, reading edition, and media section were inspected on phone and desktop layouts. Darby’s independent checks also covered short landscape, returning preferences, and the unchanged game modules.

We used WCAG 2.2 to inform focus, comfort, and control design. A full assistive-technology audit, real iPhone testing, listening evaluation, and testing with invited friends remain worthwhile next checks.
