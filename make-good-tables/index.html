Choose something small enough to try. It could be a meeting, a gathering, a class or a project.

02 · Second person

Bring something
we don’t have yet.

Invite the other person to speak or type. Listen before revising. They do not have to disagree to add something important.

You can pass. Nothing must be disclosed to continue.
Exploring on your own?

You can rehearse, but don’t invent someone’s response and treat it as theirs. Leave this blank and come back with a person, or use the clearly labeled worked example.

First person: try describing what you heard. Let the other person correct your understanding.

03 · Both people

What could we make
with both?

Look for a new possibility that takes both contributions seriously. No need to split the difference, force agreement or give up a boundary.

A record of the conversation, not a score. A changed idea, a clearer question or a named disagreement can each give you something useful to work with.

What changed in Version 2?

The first sketch illustrated prepared outcomes. This version leaves the outcome unwritten, invites a real second contribution, preserves both starting points, and records learning separately for each person. A worked example is explicitly fictional. A pause is a legitimate ending.

How this relates to Make Good Tables

Your outline’s three questions concern purpose, difference and making a good table. This small proposed exercise connects those questions through one conversation. It does not supply your navigation tool, parables, group rotations or survey method.

Before expanding, we’d like to test it with you and a small willing group. No institutional partnership or finalized curriculum is implied.

Ideas that informed the sketch
  • Your Real Discussion interview: discovery and uncertainty in learning.
  • Your TIME excerpt: needing others, being needed and being changed.
  • Your Observer essay: doing difficult things together while retaining difference.

These sources informed our interpretation; the interface and prompts are proposed adaptations, not an authorized statement of your method.
