If your prototype failed and you can say exactly why, that's a great presentation. A prototype's job is to prove one idea, not to be finished.

Your five minutes

  • Show it running. Don't describe it; play it.
  • All four of you speak. That's about 75 seconds each, so decide before 10:13 who covers what.
  • Hard stop at five minutes. There's a visible timer.
  • Then five minutes of feedback from the room.

How we give feedback

I like…Something that's working, and why it works.
I wish…Something that got in the way of the idea.
What if…One thing to try next.

Talk about the work, be specific, and keep it kind. The room is answering one question: is the idea proven or not?

Running order

  1. 10:00Opening and running order.
  2. 10:13Critique: Team U, then S, then C. Five minutes showing, five minutes of feedback.
  3. 11:13Ten-minute break. Take all ten and leave the room.
  4. 11:23Fix what the room found. Gordon visits each team: U 11:25 · S 11:45 · C 12:05.
  5. 12:30Wrap-up. Each team names one change it's making because of something another team did.

Fix-it ideas for the second half

  • Re-stage the shot in the engine, or move the camera.
  • Re-light it: lights, materials, post-process.
  • Adjust shaders, particles or timing.
  • Re-render, or re-cut the sequence.
