SESSION 02 · August 26 · First meeting · shot scale

Choose what we can see.

One person. Five frames. What do you gain as you get closer, and what do you leave out?

Extreme wide: the setting takes most of the frame. Try a closer view.

BEFORE YOU TOUCH

Make a prediction.

Before changing the frame, predict which view will help you understand the place. Which will help you read a small gesture?

SESSION 03 · August 31 · Color & lighting

Give color a purpose.

Keep the arrangement. Change the palette. Then change saturation on its own. A feeling is a reading you can explain, not a color law.

Daylight palette. Arrangement stays fixed.

BEFORE YOU TOUCH

Make a prediction.

Predict what will happen to the scene when you remove its color. What still guides your eye?

Optional. Notes stay on this page until you save a copy. Nothing is graded or submitted.

NAME IT WHEN IT HELPS

Your tools for this session

Color script

Course vocabulary

A sequence of color plans showing how palette and mood change across a story.

Palette

Course vocabulary

A selected range of colors used together.

Warm / cool

Course vocabulary

Color relationships often described as reds, oranges and yellows versus blues and greens. Meanings depend on context and culture.

Lighting

Course vocabulary

The direction, color and quality of light in a scene.

Saturation

Practice helper · supports this lesson

The intensity of a color, from gray to vivid.

Value

Practice helper · supports this lesson

How light or dark a color is, independent of its hue.

High-key / low-key

Practice helper · supports this lesson

High-key lighting usually has low contrast and few deep shadows. Low-key lighting has strong contrast and substantial shadow.

Motivated / stylized light

Practice helper · supports this lesson

Motivated light suggests a source in the world. Stylized light may serve an expressive purpose without a visible source.

Hard / soft light

Practice helper · supports this lesson

Hard light produces sharper shadow edges; soft light produces gradual edges.

Color and culture

Practice helper · supports this lesson

A color can carry different associations for different people and contexts. State your reading and evidence.

SESSION 04 · September 2 · Camera movement & perspective

Move for a reason.

A turn and a journey are different. Try a small camera diagram. See what moves before deciding what it means.

Pan: the camera turns from one position. Slide to turn it.

BEFORE YOU TOUCH

Make a prediction.

Which move changes the camera’s position? Which changes only the direction it points? Predict, then try both.

Optional. Notes stay on this page until you save a copy. Nothing is graded or submitted.

NAME IT WHEN IT HELPS

Your tools for this session

Pan

Course vocabulary

Turn the camera left or right from a fixed position.

Tilt

Course vocabulary

Turn the camera up or down from a fixed position.

Dolly

Practice helper · supports this lesson

Move the camera through space toward or away from a subject. This changes perspective; a zoom changes the lens view without moving the camera.

Crane / boom

Course vocabulary

Raise or lower the camera’s position through space.

Tracking / following

Practice helper · supports this lesson

Travel with a moving subject. A pan may follow a subject too, but the camera itself stays in one place.

Dutch angle

Course vocabulary

Roll the frame so its horizon is tilted. Different from a tilt, which points the camera up or down.

Parallax

Course vocabulary

Nearby and faraway objects appear to shift by different amounts as the viewpoint moves.

Focal length

Practice helper · supports this lesson

A lens property affecting angle of view. Perspective also depends on camera position; a longer lens alone does not change that position.

Handheld / locked-off

Practice helper · supports this lesson

Handheld includes movement from a held camera. Locked-off keeps the camera fixed. Either can serve a deliberate purpose.

Motivated camera move

Practice helper · supports this lesson

A move that serves something in the scene, such as following an action or revealing information.

Depth of field

Course vocabulary

The range of distances that appears sharp. Explore changing sharpness without moving objects in the composition studio in Session6.

TAKE IT INTO YOUR WORLD

One small transfer.

Watch a moving shot. Finish: “The camera moves so that we can see ___.” Then imagine holding it still. What would we lose or gain?

SESSION 07 · September 14 · Editing, pacing & montage

Make time mean something.

Keep the four shots. Change their durations. Then hold durations equal and change order. One variable at a time helps you see why an edit feels different.

NAME IT WHEN IT HELPS

Your tools for this session

Cut

Practice helper · supports this lesson

A change from one shot to the next.

Pacing

Course vocabulary

The rhythm and speed of the unfolding sequence.

Montage

Course vocabulary

Meaning made through a sequence of images placed together; it can compress time and need not be fast.

Continuity

Course vocabulary

Consistency across shots that helps a viewer follow space, time or action.

Beat

Course vocabulary

A meaningful moment or rhythm unit.

Cutaway

Course vocabulary

A shot away from the main action, often supplying a detail or reaction.

Wipe

Course vocabulary

A transition with a moving boundary that reveals the next image.

Quick cut

Course vocabulary

A short-duration shot within a sequence. Its effect depends on context, movement and sound.
