Want to keep your thought? Two optional notes
Compare the reasoning after you try

A long wait may create anticipation, give us time to scan the scene, or simply feel slow. Holding arrival and connection gives that interaction more screen time. Neither guarantees a particular feeling. Moving the open gate to the beginning changes what information arrives first; it may create a question rather than show a cause followed by a result.

Try explaining the effect without naming an emotion first: “The arrival takes four seconds here and one second there.” Then describe what that difference did for you.

Seven tools for talking about edits

Beat
A moment or rhythm unit in narrative; a pause or pivot that carries meaning. Course example ↗
Continuity
Visual and narrative consistency across shots; the illusion that a scene is continuous despite cuts and changes in perspective. Course example ↗
Cutaway
A shot of something other than the main action, used to provide context, reaction, or temporal information. Course example ↗
Montage
A sequence of shots whose ordering creates meaning through juxtaposition and can compress time. A montage does not have to be fast. Course example ↗
Pacing
The rhythm and speed of cuts, movement, and narrative progression; creates tension, relaxation, or emotional tempo. Course example ↗
Quick Cut
A succession of shots with short durations. It can increase perceived energy, depending on sound, movement and context. Course example ↗
Wipe
A transition in which an image progressively replaces another across the screen. Course example ↗
