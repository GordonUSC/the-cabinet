COURSE ASSIGNMENT · SEPARATE FROM OPTIONAL STUDIO PRACTICE

One World,
Two Media.

Visual Analysis Video · 10% of final grade
Individual · 3–5 minutes · Due Thursday September24, 5PM Pacific

One World, Two Media. A 3 to 5 minute video essay in which you take ONE world that exists in two forms and compare how each version does its work. Show both, talk over both, and answer three questions: what survived the crossing unchanged, what had to be reinvented because the new medium could not carry the original, and what is simply gone. That third question is the hard one and it is the one that earns the top band. You may use a pair from the course (The Last of Us game and series, League of Legends and Arcane, Minecraft and the film, Hamilton on stage and on screen) or bring your own, subject to approval in Discord. Screen recording with your own voice over it. The production value is not assessed; the seeing is. Due Thu 24 Sep, 5:00 PM PT.

Survived

What remained across the two forms?

Reinvented

What changed because the medium changed?

Gone

What could not come across, and what evidence supports that claim?

Visual Observation

5 · Excellent

Identifies specific camera work, composition, color, lighting, and movement; observations are precise and on-screen annotated

4 · Good

Names most visual elements; annotation is present but sometimes generic

3 · Fair

Identifies some visual elements; annotation is minimal

2 · Below Expectation

Vague or missing visual analysis; little or no annotation

Comparative Argument

5 · Excellent

Names what survived, what was reinvented, and what is gone, with specific on-screen evidence for each. Course terminology is used accurately and naturally throughout. The claim about what is missing is argued rather than asserted

4 · Good

All three questions answered with evidence; terminology accurate; the argument about absence is present but thinner

3 · Fair

Two of the three questions answered; comparison is mostly description rather than argument; some terminology misused

2 · Below Expectation

Describes two works without comparing them, or compares without evidence; little or no terminology

Scoring: Total possible =10 points. Two criteria ×5 points each. Averaged if multiple videos are submitted.
