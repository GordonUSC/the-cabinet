The long version

What happened, in one page

He started in 2001 because Los Angeles has an entire subculture built around game show casting, and once you are in it, you stay in it. His words: "Game shows, ever since I got on Weakest Link, I get cast." And: "I lose game shows in dramatic but awesome ways."

The Russian Roulette money bought a walk on role on Six Feet Under, season three, episode three, at an Outfest charity auction. He plays a student in Claire's art class. That is the whole chain: a trapdoor on cable television, to a queer film festival, to HBO.

Four years later he came back to GSN. Starface, September 2006, a celebrity-photo trivia game hosted by Danny Bonaduce. Gordon introduced himself as a video game consultant from Reston, Virginia, who had once ridden an elevator with Greg Brady, then beat Nicole and Julia in the main game 1,300 to 850 to 450 and walked out with nine hundred from the bonus round. That makes nine confirmed television appearances against his own lifetime count of eleven, and it fits neatly between Russian Roulette and Merv Griffin's Crosswords.

The worst night was Million Dollar Mind Game. His team of six ran to $360,000 without spending a single help, then banked $600,000. Five of the six voted to take the money. One teammate voted to play on, and the rules say one vote is enough to force the whole team forward. They missed the next two questions and went home with nothing. Buzzerblog named it the most memorable game show moment of 2011.
