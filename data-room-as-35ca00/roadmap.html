Part 1

You already qualify

Part-time appointments ... are appropriate for individuals who are asked to teach a course because of special expertise and who have another full-time position or career. USC Faculty Handbook, section 4-B(3)(b). The word USC uses for this is "adjunct."
Teaching experience is welcomed but not required. From a School of Cinematic Arts adjunct posting dated October 14, 2026. Its floor: a bachelor's degree and 7 to 9 years in the industry. You have an MBA, a CPA and about 18 years.

You would not be the first operator in the room. The USC Games faculty page lists Laird Malamed, founding COO and CFO of Oculus, as an Adjunct Professor, and Robert Nashak, a former studio COO, teaching the business of games. It also lists a working attorney from a game company who teaches video game law. That page is not a live roster, so read it as precedent. The path from the operating side of a studio to a USC classroom is an established one.

Likely title: Adjunct Lecturer or Adjunct Assistant ProfessorUsual load: one course a yearYou keep your day job. That is the point of the title.

Part 4

Your course, sketched

A starting point to argue with, not a finished syllabus. Two units means one evening a week, 100 minutes, for 15 weeks.

Proposed, 2 units

The Business of a Game Studio

How a studio is funded, budgeted, staffed and kept healthy, taught by someone who has sat on both sides of the deal.

Weeks 1 to 3Where the money comes from

Who pays for a game and why. Reading a studio's profit and loss. What a greenlight is, seen from the publisher's chair and the developer's.

Weeks 4 to 6Deals and budgets

Publishing and platform terms in plain language. Building a budget from a headcount plan. Cash planning month by month.

Week 7Midterm: pitch a budget

Each team defends a one-page budget for a small game to a panel playing the publisher.

Weeks 8 to 10Operations is a craft

Production as an operating system. Co-development and outsourcing partners. Culture, autonomy and why people stay.

Weeks 11 to 12Hiring across borders

How headcount gets approved. Recruiting, relocation and visas. Running a team in several countries.

Weeks 13 to 14How studios are valued

What an acquirer looks at. Valuation basics for creative companies. Managing risk when plans change.

Week 15Final: your studio's first year

A first-year operating plan: team, budget, milestones and one page on what could go wrong. USC requires a final summative experience in the scheduled finals slot.
